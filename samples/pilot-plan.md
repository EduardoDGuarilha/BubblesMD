# A smaller first release

We want people to feel confident when they ask for help with their writing. The first pilot should tell us where the experience helps and where it becomes confusing.

## Run a focused pilot

For the first release, we will invite twenty volunteers to try the editor for one week. Each person will write a short document, request changes to a selected passage, and decide which suggestions to keep. We will collect their feedback after each session.

The goal is to prove that the editor can handle every writing task. We will track how many suggestions are generated and how much time people spend in the app. We will also ask whether the suggestions helped them express what they meant.

## Listen before expanding

After the sessions, we will look for moments when people hesitate, lose track of a suggestion, or accept something they did not intend to change. We will improve those moments before inviting a larger group.

- Watch where people **pause** before accepting.
- Note every *surprising* change.
- Read the [pilot checklist](https://example.com/pilot) before each session.

A useful suggestion is only part of the experience. People should understand what changed, stay in control of the result, and return to their writing without losing their train of thought.

> Keep the pilot small enough that we can talk to every participant.

## Decide what comes next

At the end of the pilot, we will add more features based on what participants tell us. The next release should address the clearest problem we observed.
